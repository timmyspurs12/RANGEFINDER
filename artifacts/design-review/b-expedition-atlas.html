IDENTITY — “EXPEDITION ATLAS”

  • A living expedition journal: warm chart-paper ground, ink rules, a layered paper-cut Earth with visible thickness (shadow duplicates), and a vermilion measuring tape wrapped around the equator — the tape IS the game’s range.
  • Depth from paper layering: soft heavy shadows, chips and cards that sit above the page, slightly rotated elements like things pinned by hand. Nothing symmetrical or dashboard-like.
  • Typography: journal serif for prose and the question, hand-stamped Arial caps for labels, tabular numerals on paper chits.

INTERACTION APPROACH

  • The range is a physical act: you stretch a tape between two large brass map fasteners; your bounds are inked on paper chits that tilt like hand-pinned notes.
  • Lock = pressing a wax seal (the “R” stamp) next to a vermilion button — sealing your estimate.

MOTION

  • Drag: fastener presses down, tape stretches between them, chits travel with the fasteners.
  • Reveal: postmark slams in with overshoot; a brass pin drops with two expanding sonar rings; on a miss a dashed slack-thread shows the shortfall with a ×factor chit; score card presses in, count-up settles on the exact server value.

EMBEDDED FIT (719×545)

  • Splash: masthead 60 + question 120 + globe 246 + CTA block 110 — fits; the artwork carries the question’s meaning (taping the equator) with zero callout labels.
  • Game: header 90 + ruler strip 130 + seal row 90 + how-to 30; large 48 px fasteners and 32 px tape keep touch targets generous.
Trade-offs: warmest and most playful of the three; slightly closer to “illustrated quiz” territory than A, and layered shadows need care at very narrow widths. Its tape/pin vocabulary could donate itself to A’s reveal if you want more warmth there.
